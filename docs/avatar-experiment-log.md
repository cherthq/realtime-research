# Chert avatar experiments

## What runs today

The private GPT-Live path uses Marin for speech and AvatarForever for synchronized video. On the research H100 PCIe, Brick Room runs at 512 × 384 in BF16 with a trained two-step sampler and a 24 FPS publication cap. Sama with ElevenLabs remains the comparison path. Both share capacity for one active avatar conversation. The browser and FaceTime are two ways into that capacity.

## What we learned

| Study | Result | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reference portraits and continuity, October–November | Keeping the original portrait, first chunk and generated history across sentences removed a source of visible resets. Long sequences could still change body scale, color or identity. The audited checkpoint had a zero image-conditioning projection, although other conditioning paths remained active. | Keep continuous history, the 4:3 frame and the original portrait. Stability over a full call remains a visual check. |
| Idle mouth and first words | A strong listening prompt reduced false mouth movement but also weakened articulation when speech started. Listening on silent PCM with a stronger speaking cue at onset preserved more of the transition. Portrait and seed still mattered. | Keep the listening/onset controls. Do not describe the avatar as always closed-mouth during silence. |
| Voice delivery and Qwen adaptation | A 288-generation comparison varied plain replies, tone prefixes and conversational prompts. Tone prefixes were learnable but introduced factual and memory errors. OpenYap supplied human speech patterns, including disfluencies that sometimes made answers stutter. | Judge complete conversations and first words, alongside fluency and latency. Text training and TTS controls address different problems. |
| Targeted Sama fine-tuning | A rank-16 LoRA used 491 examples, including 44 reviewed OpenYap windows, retained capabilities, authored dialogue, and tool use. Some context and tool decisions improved. Shortened answers lost information the caller wanted. | Keep original Sama as the local text baseline. No general preference win was established. |
| Duplex models on one H100 | Haru and PersonaPlex were evaluated as speech alternatives. With PersonaPlex active the initial shared run generated about 13–14 new avatar frames/s. A later run reached 16.66 distinct frames/s with 293 holds. | Use hosted speech for the private GPT-Live path. A video publication rate of 24 FPS can include repeated frames. |
| Paired footage, adapters and distillation | The team qualified time-aligned video separately from OpenYap audio. A 19,376-parameter mouth residual reduced falsely open frames from 102 to 82 on one held-out recorded-audio comparison. Visible mouth artifacts and missed restarts remained. A shorter-onset output-head experiment failed its held-out screen. | Preserve the faster trained two-step sampler as a private trial. The mouth and short-onset candidates are separate experiments. |
| Cache, render shape, gaze and exposure | Cache-off runs sometimes looked more stable but cost throughput. In a matched H100 replay, 16-frame chunks gained a median 133 ms against 32, below the 250 ms target, with three long receiver gaps in one call. Eye adjustment and brightness control had visible quality or speed tradeoffs. | Keep 32-frame steady chunks. Evaluate new-frame rate, drift, gaze, seams and exposure on received video. |
| Interruption and listening handoff | Invalidating stale speech prevents queued speaking motion from playing silently. A recovery experiment shortened near-identical video from a median 1,108 to 806 ms, while the median audible tail moved from 273 to 284 ms. | Keep cancellation and media epochs aligned. Faster visual recovery alone does not qualify an audio interruption change. |
| Memory, vision and tool use | Tests covered remembered names, corrections, camera revocation, unavailable evidence, grouped captions, search interruptions and call cleanup. Similar-looking prompts could still select the wrong tool. | Keep state per call, inspect a current shared camera frame only on request, and attach sources to played answers. |
| Latency and transport | The Sama control measured 4.011 s end-of-question to audible reply over eight turns. A narrower listening margin measured 4.415 s and early drafting 4.140 s. A separate GPT-Live paired pacing test gained only 127 ms median. | Keep regular pacing. GPU render time, first provider audio, browser playback and physical handset latency are different measurements. |
| GPT-Live against Sama, November 25 | With the same three synthetic turns, GPT-Live measured 4.61 s median to audible output, Sama 3.99 s. GPT-Live recalled the collar color; Sama asked for a camera. The interruption test measured 1.70 s for GPT-Live and 0.66 s for Sama. | Use GPT-Live for the product-style voice trial. Three turns do not establish an overall latency or quality ranking. |
| Document and web retrieval, November 25 | GPT-Live's earlier deployment disabled external tools. A browser replay of the fix retrieved the 30-second queue rule, looked up GTA VI without an explicit search command, and declined to invent a missing portrait detail. The web acknowledgment took 6.5 s; one caption was partial and the longest video gap was about 6 s. | Verified on the private browser preview. Playback stalls and incomplete captions remain open. |
| FaceTime transport, November 25 | Chert's named-agent doctor dispatched the worker in 0.9 s. A synthetic caller in a separate LiveKit room published microphone audio and received 198 video frames and audible output. The GPU slot was released after disconnect. | Two separate inbound FaceTime calls reached the agent. Chert reported healthy outbound audio and video on both. Handset-perceived latency, lip-sync and incoming camera access still need a phone-side check. |

Early work also covered the portrait gallery, camera sharing, mobile 4:3 layout, captions, queue admission, voice casting, and setup/rollback. Later studies used synthetic caller recordings and selected portraits to make comparisons repeatable. The reported timings describe the individual experiments, not a production service-level guarantee. Private media, dataset samples, voice assets and model weights are excluded from this report.

## Additional experiment groups

- **OpenYap scale and retention.** The bounded OpenYap-only 2B run and regularized follow-up failed the quality screen. Regularization avoided observed repetition loops but passed 3 of 12 fresh capability checks, against 7 of 12 for original 2B. Dataset size alone did not solve conversational relevance.
- **Conversation preference and repair.** Studies compared direct wording, turn grouping, length, examples, formatting, decoder suffixes, draft anchors and preference-ranked replies. Pairwise training could change candidate rankings without improving complete conversations. Corrections, unfinished answers and memory need their own review.
- **Turn taking.** Separate experiments covered conventional and semantic endpoints, pauses inside sentences, overlap, backchannels, manual interruption and stale tool returns. A faster stop detector can cut off a caller mid-sentence. The timing of the cutoff at the browser matters even when backend cancellation succeeds.
- **Retrieval and camera.** Prepared lookup, native tool selection, CPU versus hosted routing, evidence budgets, and fresh versus stale frames were exercised on ordinary and interrupted turns. Successful retrieval, the words actually spoken and the citations actually delivered are three separate checks.
- **Media and lifecycle.** Raw PCM, native PCM, RTC loopback, receiver pacing, startup recovery, admission, and cleanup experiments separated provider output from audio actually played. Single-session ownership and idempotent cleanup protect the next caller when a start, interruption or teardown is retried.
- **Evaluation.** Private blind comparisons, short scripted conversations, full-source dialogue adaptation, timing traces and physical listening answer different questions. Synthetic calls support repeatable regressions. They cannot establish that a person prefers a voice, that gaze looks natural, or that FaceTime will sound the same on a handset.
